# 0.1 — Reactor path experiment

## Goal
Tune a single particle, representing a beam, into a stable closed trajectory that activates several targets evenly without crossing cooling zones. The path is a continuously revised finite prediction, not a prescribed curve. Guidance should detect the loop the field almost supports and complete it, working like a magnetic rail that the beam rides — without teleporting the beam or attaching it to the wrong branch of a self-crossing trajectory.

## 0.1.1 Runtime and architecture
- Open `index.html` directly: classic scripts, local assets, no dependencies or build.
- Separate deterministic simulation (`js/reactor.js`) from canvas rendering and input (`js/app.js`). Export the simulation conditionally for Node tests.
- Use a fixed simulation timestep, bounded catch-up, reusable forecast buffers, and a logical arena independent of viewport size.
- Provide pause, single-step, reset, and forecast/guidance controls for investigation.

## 0.1.2 Particle dynamics and forecast
- Begin with a weak anisotropic restoring field producing orbit-like motion. This is a gameplay model, not a physical reactor or Lorentz-force simulation.
- Add softened, bounded magnet forces. Magnets move along the perimeter; orientation changes their field; polarity switches attraction/repulsion.
- Store position, velocity, and absolute time for every forecast sample. Refresh the 14-second forecast every 0.6 seconds using the same integrator and collision rules as live motion.
- Resolve forecast/guidance feedback with two prediction passes through reusable buffers: one pass proposes a route and the second follows that proposal. Never mutate the live particle while forecasting.

## 0.1.3 Loop-lock guidance
- Detect the loop on an unguided forecast pass, so detection never depends on the guide's own limited authority. A candidate is a near-recurrence anchored at the beam: an earlier forecast state within the detection radius of the beam and a later state returning to it inside detection tolerances, at least a configurable minimum lap period apart. The rail is the beam's own next lap, rolled forward as the beam moves.
- Commit the best candidate as the rail and stitch its seam: blend the closure error into the lap so the rail is one closed curve. An unstitched seam is wider than the pull tolerance, and the beam would cross the junction unguided every lap.
- Pull toward the nearest compatible section of the rail — close and already moving the beam's way — and match local speed and heading, aiming slightly ahead on the rail so the beam joins the flow instead of braking against it. A wrong-way branch at a crossing can never capture the beam.
- The pull acts only inside the player's match tolerances (distance, direction): full authority up to a fraction of each tolerance, smooth cutoff to exactly zero at the tolerance. Outside them the guide is exactly idle — it completes loops the field almost supports, it never drags the beam onto an impossible orbit.
- Hold the rail with hysteresis: while the beam rides it, freeze the committed shape and only refresh the grace timer, so a better-scoring loop elsewhere cannot hijack the ride. Re-lock onto the best new candidate only after the beam has stayed off the rail past the grace period; an unrelated loop may capture the beam only when the forecast shows the beam reaching its entry within a short window.
- Run a second guided forecast pass following the committed loop, so the displayed forecast matches the forces the beam actually feels. Never snap position or velocity.

## 0.1.4 Reflectors and controls
- Provide short, rotatable segments in the chamber. Reflect only when the incoming direction forms a shallow angle with the surface; steep impacts pass through.
- Detect swept crossings to avoid tunneling and move through the remaining timestep after reflection.
- Select a magnet or reflector on canvas; drag magnets along the frame. Rotate the selection using a slider or wheel. Provide keyboard alternatives and visible selection feedback.
- Guidance controls: strength, pull distance tolerance, direction tolerance, movement matching, and minimum loop period.

## 0.1.5 Activation and cooling
- Targets and cooling zones are transparent to particle motion.
- Compute segment-circle overlap and multiply by timestamp differences to integrate dwell time, rather than counting samples or entries.
- Forecast target duty cycles and cooling exposure over the finite horizon. Display signed percent deviation from each target's requested duty cycle next to it.
- Track actual target activation with a time-smoothed dwell signal. Keep actual and predicted signals distinct.
- Require sustained acceptable live activation and cooling exposure for stable status.

## 0.1.6 Presentation and validation
- Dark instrument-panel presentation with clear trajectory, beam, zones, controls, target bars, and responsive high-DPI canvas.
- Debug overlay must show the algorithm's actual state: the committed loop rail, green ring at the aim point on the rail, green rail movement vector, cyan current movement vector, dashed offset to the rail, white live force, amber forecast forces, and numeric loop period/offset/movement/force values.
- Hide only sub-visual force arrows, never the target or movement state; retain exact numeric force in the readout.
- Automated checks: determinism, finite bounded motion, forecast state isolation, loop detection and its gates, seam stitching into a closed rail, rail pull direction, tolerance cutoffs and fades, wrong-way rejection, ride hysteresis, forecast movement buffers, dwell geometry, and grazing reflection.
- Deterministic experiment: compare recurrence in position-plus-velocity space with guidance disabled and enabled. Browser smoke: direct-file loading, rendering, controls, debug readout, pause/reset, resizing, and no runtime errors.

## 0.1.7 Follow-up tuning
- Swept guide strength against pull tolerance with the loop-lock guide: at the player's 40 px / 30° tolerances, strength 0.9 with whole-lap seam stitching and ride hysteresis locks the beam to a lap ~96% of the time (recurrence median ~5 px); without guidance the median stays ~130 px. Defaults set accordingly.
- Next: playtest magnet edits against a locked loop, ship at least one known-stable magnet preset, and calibrate target duties and difficulty.
- Establish a solvable scenario and calibrated target duties before adding presets, difficulty levels, or persistence.
- Profile periodic double-pass forecasting before considering spatial indexing, workers, or dependencies.

## 0.1.8 Review, recovery boundaries, and preset search
- Fix the validation harness: apply a test perturbation exactly once (a time-window guard fired on two consecutive 120 Hz steps and silently doubled the edit), print deviations correctly, and report a perturbation run per phase window instead of one aggregate median. With the documented edit the guide re-fits once at the edit and recovers (recurrence median 1.0 px, locked 100%); a much larger simultaneous edit leaves the field without a lap the pull can complete and the guide churns through rail families — the guide's design boundary, reported as a separate probe.
- Provide a lean evaluation mode for batch experiments: `skipDisplayForecast` skips the display forecast pass while live dynamics stay bit-identical (pinned by a test) and makes a 40-second evaluation about ten times cheaper.
- Search for a shippable preset with `experiments/preset-search.js`: deterministic parallel coordinate descent over UI-reachable layouts (perimeter positions, whole-degree angles, 0.05 strength steps, reflector angles), scored by the worst live deviation across several cycles, the cooling spike, the ride wander, engagement, and rail churn; rounds resume from earlier search logs; candidates are rounded to ship granularity and confirmed at full fidelity over 300 seconds.
- Measured obstacle: locked orbits that feed the targets on searched fields breathe — each lap's target duty swings ±40% with a 60–120 s period — so the stability streak keeps resetting, while the default field's stationary lock feeds only one target. Remedies tested and rejected: guide gains, a higher force cap, forced rail refresh, and rail rolling (rolling removes the ride wander, 21 → 4 px, but the duty swing grows, 46% → 82% — the swing is the lap geometry, not tracking error). The next search must select for stationary lap geometry; `experiments/logs/` records every round.

## 0.1.9 Edit continuity and fast-forward
- Field edits must be continuous: a magnet or reflector nudge reshapes the orbit by about the size of the nudge. Re-deriving the rail from a forecast of the beam's live state does not do that, because the guided state has already been pulled off the unguided trajectory, so the best near-recurrence of that forecast is regularly a *different* lap and a sub-pixel edit swaps the orbit.
- On a field edit while the beam rides the rail, transport the held lap instead of re-fitting it: propagate the rail's own entry state through the edited field for one period, stitch the seam, and keep that lap when the beam still meets the transported rail inside the pull tolerances. The rail then follows the edit, and detection tolerances are not re-litigated per edit.
- When the edited field moves the orbit out from under the beam, the transported rail no longer meets the tolerances and the search re-derives the rail from the beam's own next lap — the large-edit path is unchanged. A held lap with no fresh candidate nearby is likewise transported rather than frozen.
- Fast-forward: a speed slider (1x to 50x) multiplies simulated time per real second on top of the unchanged fixed 120 Hz step. The forecast refresh is a real-time cadence, so its simulation-time interval stretches with the speed: fast-forward then costs about the same per second as normal speed, the display refresh rate is unchanged, and the rail re-derives on the same real-time rhythm.
- Checks: determinism, a sub-pixel edit that keeps the beam on its orbit, a small edit that moves the rail by about the edit and not by a lap, an edit that breaks the lap and forces a re-derivation, and the refresh interval stretching with the speed.

## 0.1.12 Auto fuel and overheat cone lift
- Add an **auto fuel** toggle to the reactor plant: while checked, each simulation step clears burned-out fuel circles (fuel at 0) and tops the fuel count up to a player-set number — the count from the last **+ Fuel** press, or the initial 3 by default. The number is clamped to the fuel maximum. The policy lives on the sim (`autoFuel`, `autoFuelTarget`) and runs inside the deterministic step, so runs stay reproducible; reset turns it off and restores the default number.
- Emulate lifting fuel cones on overheat: the fuel circle's live size is full while heat is at or below 100% and shrinks linearly to zero as heat rises from 100% to 120%. The shrunk size is the circle's gameplay size — beam dwell, click-to-remove, and drawing all use it — so a hot reactor absorbs less fuel heat until it cools. Heat may overheat past a full buffer up to the 120% ceiling; the heat bar caps at full and turns red past 100% while the number keeps counting.
- Checks: the cone size factor and live radius across the heat range, overheat feeding and its cutoff at the ceiling, click-to-remove at the shrunk size, auto clear/top-up/clamp/default/reset, plus the existing suite, UI wiring, and smoke coverage.
