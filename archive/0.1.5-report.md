### 2. Why the Previous Implementation (0.1.4) Does Not Form a Closed Loop

In `0.1.4`, guidance was rebuilt around **Route Memory** (`planLag = 1.8 s`) and a **timestamp-seeded projection**:
1. Every 1.8 seconds, a 14-second forward extrapolation was frozen as a committed route `reference`.
2. At every simulation step $t$, the algorithm computed `seedIndex(route, 0) = Math.round((t - route.t[0]) / DT)` and searched **only** within a tight window of $\approx 0.25\text{ s}$ around that scheduled timestamp.

#### The Consequence
Because the lookup was strictly tied to the particle’s **elapsed clock time**:
- When the particle completes a full circuit around the reaction chamber (e.g., after ~4 seconds) and flies through the same spatial region $(x, y)$, the algorithm looks for route nodes at **$t \approx 4\text{ s}$**, **not** at $t \approx 0\text{ s}$.
- It is mathematically forbidden from seeing or matching the track of its previous lap.
- If the unguided physical field precesses or drifts by 10–15 px per lap (which generic anisotropic Lissajous curves naturally do), the particle simply follows the drifted prediction.
- When the 1.8 s memory expires, a new drifting open-ended path is frozen.

**Summary**: The 0.1.4 system is a follower of a sequence of open-ended trajectory snapshots, not a self-closing orbital attractor.

### 3. What is "Movement Matching" and How Does It Work?

In physics and control theory, turning an open or quasi-periodic trajectory into a **stable closed loop** (a limit cycle) requires a **phase-space attractor**.

Instead of matching a clock timestamp $t$ on a timeline, **Movement Matching** matches the particle's spatial state $(\vec{p}, \vec{v})$ in space:

#### How It Works:
1. **Spatial Search Across Loops**:
   Given particle position $\vec{p}$ and velocity $\vec{v}$, search the trajectory (either the full forward forecast or a ring buffer of recent flight history) for any segment that satisfies:
   - **Spatial proximity**: $\|\vec{p} - \vec{x}_{\text{segment}}\| \le 40\text{ px}$.
   - **Heading alignment**: $\angle(\vec{v}, \vec{v}_{\text{segment}}) \le 30^\circ$.
   - **Loop separation**: $\Delta t \ge 2.0\text{ s}$ (or $\Delta s \ge 250\text{ px}$ along the curve), so it ignores the current instantaneous point and only detects **other laps/cycles** passing through the same area.
2. **Phase-Space Pull**:
   When such a segment is found, apply two lateral components:
   - **Cross-track attraction**: $\vec{F}_{\perp} = -k_p \cdot d_{\perp} \hat{n}$ (pulls position onto the track).
   - **Velocity alignment torque**: $\vec{F}_{\theta} = k_v \cdot (\hat{v}_{\text{track}} - \hat{v})$ (rotates velocity vector to match track heading).
3. **Loop Closure Convergence**:
   - On **Lap 1**, the particle traces path $L_1$.
   - On **Lap 2**, as the particle returns near the starting area, it detects $L_1$ within 40 px and 30°.
   - Movement Matching pulls Lap 2 directly into the groove of $L_1$.
   - Because error decays by a factor $|\lambda| < 1$ on each pass, the trajectory converges in 1–2 revolutions into an exact, stationary, self-repeating **closed loop**!
   - When the player adjusts a magnet, the loop smoothly deforms to a new equilibrium. If adjusted strongly, the particle diverges beyond 40 px / 30°, breaks free, and snaps into a new closed orbit.
