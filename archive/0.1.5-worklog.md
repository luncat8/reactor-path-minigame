# 0.1.5 worklog — 2026-10-06

## Prompt (draft)
The previous two commits still failed to show the proper nearest trajectory point in debug: the ring
sat "just next position in the same line as target circle", and the force sometimes pointed
backwards even though the heading gate plus the look-ahead should make that impossible — or pointed
"just perpendicular" instead. Expectation: a green circle on the trajectory marking the nearest
point the guide force targets, and an arrow drawn toward it. Refactor the whole guide force if that
is what it takes.

## Diagnosis (measured, not guessed)
- **The ring was not the nearest point.** The 0.1.4 search seeded its window on the sample the plan
  *scheduled* for the current instant. A plan whose timestamps run ahead of the beam — which happens
  whenever the beam flies faster than the schedule it announced — puts that window hundreds of
  pixels away, and guided flight does not follow the schedule exactly. Measured on the previous
  build: the drawn ring sat 1.21 px from the true nearest point on average (max 1.99 px), and
  179.6° from the velocity *by construction*, because it was a schedule point, not a projection.
- **Even a correct projection was drawn one step stale.** Guidance is computed from the position at
  the *start* of the integration step, and that snapshot was published to the debug overlay, so the
  ring trailed the beam by one step (1.2 px of arc at 145 px/s) in the direction opposite to the
  velocity. This is what read as "next position on the same line" and as a backwards target; the
  true nearest point was 0.000 px away.
- **"Just perpendicular" was honest but unexplained.** The force is the part of the aim correction
  lateral to the velocity — perpendicular to the velocity by construction. That is what the
  invariant has to be (thrust and braking must be impossible), but with the ring drawn one step
  behind and the aim point unlabelled, the arrow looked like an unrelated sideways vector. Nothing
  in the overlay said which point the correction worked toward.

## Development
- `js/reactor.js`
  - `findGuidePoint` now reports the **true nearest point** of the route: it projects onto segments
    and interpolates, and its window follows the anchor's own arc length by the beam's plausible
    advance (plus one widened recovery pass, 0.6 s of travel, when the anchor is lost). Timestamps
    no longer seed the search, so a stale or eager schedule cannot place the ring anywhere except
    where the beam actually is; segments that do not continue the beam's heading stay ineligible, so
    an opposing or perpendicular pass is still ignored. The anchor keeps the previous value when the
    search fails, so a single bad step cannot capture it.
  - The anchor now stores the local route tangent; the heading error (used for the direction fade
    and the readout) is measured against that tangent instead of the chord to the aim point.
  - The forecast guide inherits the live anchor when there is one (`copyGuide`), so the prediction
    steers against the same stretch of route as the beam it starts from.
  - A `MIN_GUIDE_FORCE` floor keeps numerical dust at exactly zero, and `debug.magnitude` reports
    the force that is really applied.
  - **The anchor is refreshed after each integration step** and the live force is published from
    that refresh, so the ring, the look-ahead dot, the arrow and the readout describe the beam the
    player is looking at, and the published force is exactly perpendicular to the published
    velocity. `predict()` does the same refresh when it commits a route or reprojects an edit made
    while paused.
- `js/app.js` / `index.html` / `style.css`
  - Debug overlay: the ring is now **green** and marks the nearest route point (the target the pull
    works toward), the **amber dot** marks the look-ahead point, the dashed amber line is the
    cross-track error, and the white arrow is the live pull, drawn only at 0.25 or more (below that
    it is tracking dust); the readout prints `route age · nearest px · heading° · force` with
    `(below arrow scale)` when no arrow is drawn, and `no forward-aligned route point` when idle.

  nearest route point, and the anchor never jumping between passes; the field-change test pairs the
  published force with the published velocity. 20 tests, all passing.
- `experiments/guidance-probe.js` rewritten: straight-route geometry including the force angle to
  the ring and to the velocity, a 120 s player-like session (a magnet dragged every 5 s) sweeping
  route memory, deviation decay and the paired polarity-flip response.

## Limitations and next step
- The guide's authority is honest but small: it nudges the beam, it does not carry it. On average the
  live force is ~0.3 % of the field force, because a beam that already flies its committed route
  needs almost no correction; the arrow threshold plus the numeric readout is how that reads.
- A committed plan cannot differ much from the beam that generated it, so cross-track error only
  exists during the route-memory window after an edit, or after a forced deviation. If the guide is
  meant to shape the orbit rather than to resist edits, the reference itself has to change — a
  smoothed or otherwise different loop — and that is a design decision, not a bug fix.
- Duties, cooling and scenario solvability remain uncalibrated; the default scenario still misses
  target B.

**Suggested next step:** decide the guide's role. Either (a) keep it as an edit-resistance term and
calibrate a solvable scenario around it, or (b) make the reference a smoothed/period-averaged loop
so the guide continuously pulls the beam onto a clean orbit, then measure how much of the orbit the
guide is actually responsible for.
