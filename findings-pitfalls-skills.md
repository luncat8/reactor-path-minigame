# Findings and pitfalls

- Keep forecast and live simulation buffers separate. Forecasting must copy the particle into reusable scratch state and must not accumulate live activation or advance the live clock.
- A nearest point on the immediate continuation is mathematically correct but useless for loop formation. Exclude enough near future that selection can reach a return pass.
- At trajectory crossings, nearest position alone can choose an opposing branch or a branch with very different speed. Store movement vectors and select in phase space: position plus velocity error. Reject perpendicular and opposing states before scoring.
- Use the selected point consistently. The green ring, target movement arrow, readout, and controller must all come from the same interpolated segment state and current simulation instant.
- Position-only pursuit can arrive with the wrong heading and cross the path again. A damped controller needs both position error and target-minus-current velocity error to join a repeatable loop.
- A full state controller is not constrained to be lateral: braking or acceleration can be the correct way to match target speed. Prevent harmful forces by selecting a compatible target and bounding/fading authority, not by deleting the along-velocity component.
- A temporal dead zone needs a fade. Strictly skipping two seconds and immediately applying full force creates target handoff impulses; a one-second smoothstep gives return candidates gradual authority.
- Search only future timestamps for loop closure. Past trajectory points behave like a history rail after player edits and let the just-traversed outgoing branch compete with the intended next return branch.
- Forecast and guidance form an implicit feedback problem. Two reusable forecast passes provide a current proposal without introducing allocations or a long-lived committed route.
- Draw current movement, target movement, and applied force as separate vectors. A force arrow alone cannot explain whether the chosen trajectory branch has the right phase state.
- Debug arrows must be scaled honestly. A forced minimum length makes numerical dust look like a real pull; use a compressive scale, a visibility threshold, and an exact numeric readout.
- Target influence is dwell time, not entry count. Segment-circle overlap multiplied by timestamp differences makes forecasting less sensitive to sample spacing.
- Preserve classic script loading and guarded CommonJS exports so the same core runs under `file://` and Node tests without a build.
- Keep browser tooling external to the runtime tree. The optional Playwright experiment accepts `BROWSER_EXECUTABLE` for environments where automatic browser downloads are unavailable.
- A committed lap's closure error (median ~50 px in the default field) is usually wider than a tight pull tolerance. If the rail keeps its seam, the beam crosses the junction unguided every lap and the guide merely follows the drift. Stitch the seam into the rail before pulling.
- Stitch linearly over the whole lap rather than bending the tail: a tail-concentrated blend creates a local curvature spike the beam escapes from; a whole-lap linear blend is nearly a constant-velocity bias and keeps the rail close to the field's own dynamics.
- Re-committing the globally best-scoring recurrence every refresh lets an unrelated loop family hijack the rail mid-ride (observed 170–530 px jumps during perfect rides). Freeze the committed shape while the beam is on the rail; re-fit only after a grace period off it.
- Detect loops on the unguided forecast. Detection on the guided forecast eats its own reference: the guide only partially follows the rail, so the guided closure fails and drops the very loop being ridden.
- Pull tolerance and guide strength are coupled levers: below the natural lap-to-lap drift no strength locks, and the smallest strength that locks at a given tolerance is the right default. Sweep them together, measuring recurrence locked-fraction rather than engagement alone — engagement without lock means the rail is following the beam, not correcting it.
- To test guidance against synthetic geometry, set `sim.particle` where the synthetic route lives: loop detection is anchored at the live particle position, and circle-loop scenarios must account for the rail wrapping (a skewed beam can find a compatible section on the far side of the loop).
- A time-window mutation guard inside a fixed-step harness fires once per step inside the window: `if (t >= 40 && t < 40.02)` applied the test perturbation to two consecutive 120 Hz steps and silently doubled the edit. Apply a one-shot edit with a flag, never with a time window.
- The doubled edit above produced a fake "zombie ride" (a stale rail that the beam shadowed inside pull tolerance while laps never repeated), which invited a gate on the ride velocity error. Measurement killed the idea: healthy-ride and stale-ride velocity errors overlap once smoothed (healthy p99 ~33 px/s, stale median ~27), and every spurious unseat re-commits a different lap family — the healthy default lock collapsed from 4.8 px median / 96% locked to 124.8 px / 9%. Do not gate the ride freeze on a continuous error signal; freeze integrity is worth more than any single re-fit.
- With the documented single edit the loop-lock guide does recover: one re-commit at the edit, then recurrence median 1.0 px and 100% locked. A much larger simultaneous edit (doubled distance and rotation) leaves the field without a lap the pull can complete, and the guide churns through rail families without re-locking — that is the design boundary ("completes loops the field almost supports"), so validate recovery with the documented edit and report the boundary separately.
- Target duty arithmetic for designing a winning scenario: duty = chord / (145 px/s × lap period), so at 6.5% ±30% a lap must pass within ~25 px of a 38 px target centre at an 8.3 s period, the lap period must stay roughly 6.2–11.5 s (the chord is capped by the 76 px diameter), and a near-diametral passage is the robust choice because the chord is least sensitive to wander exactly at the centre.
- Batch searches over field layouts can skip the display forecast pass (`skipDisplayForecast`): loop detection, commitment, and live dynamics stay bit-identical (a test pins the equivalence), while an evaluation gets ~10× cheaper (40 s of simulation drop from ~3.2 s to ~0.3 s) and thousands of layouts become searchable.
- Do not optimise the wrong proxy for orbit stability. Ride wander and the unguided pair's duty both look plausible, and both are wrong: shaping on wander cut it from 19–30 px to 6 px while the duty swing stayed at 43–55%, a rolling rail cut it to 4 px while the swing grew to 82%, and the pair's duty spread was dominated by pair-selection noise (default field 67%, best candidate 20%). The only honest objective is the worst live duty deviation (the quantity that resets the stability streak) over several limit cycles.
- Locked rails on searched layouts showed a slow duty oscillation (60–120 s period, ±40%, undamped over 600 s) while the rail itself stayed frozen: the committed lap is the beam's own next return, which can sit a few percent off the field's long-term natural orbit, and the pull turns that mismatch into a beat. Verification must be long (≥ 180 s) and preset search should minimise the worst |deviation| over a long horizon, not the value at one final instant — a 90 s snapshot reads 17% exactly where a 150 s one reads −29%.
- Re-deriving the rail from a forecast of the beam's *live* state after a field edit picks a different lap: the guided state has been pulled onto the rail, which the unguided field does not reproduce, so that forecast's best near-recurrence is regularly another family (measured: best-pair periods 7.60-8.43 s against a held 8.00-8.28 s, and its lap 105-161 px from the held rail). A 2 px magnet nudge then moved the rail 46-62 px and left the beam 58-86 px off it - outside the 40 px pull tolerance - so the beam flew free and the orbit changed by up to 316 px. Transport the held lap instead: propagate the rail's own entry state through the edited field for one period, stitch the seam, and keep it while the beam still meets the transported rail inside the pull tolerances. The same nudge then moves the beam by <= 11 px.
- Do not gate a transported lap on the detection tolerances. A held lap already sits near them (transported seam 29 deg of the 45 deg direction limit and 63 px of the 100 px closure limit at zero edit), so a sub-pixel edit flips the decision and re-fits anyway. Judge the transported rail by the pull's own anchor test on the *stitched* rail - the unstitched lap can be 60+ px from the rail the beam will actually ride.
- A refresh cadence expressed in simulation seconds becomes a throughput ceiling when the UI adds a speed control: the display forecast pass alone capped the sim at ~21x real time, so a 50x slider silently ran at 21x. Express the cadence in real time and stretch its simulation-time interval with the speed (`forecastInterval = FORECAST_PERIOD * speed`); fast-forward then costs about the same per second and the on-screen refresh rate is unchanged.
- Heat that is only added by fuel and only removed by coolers is conserved, so a tuned output target cannot exceed the fuel supply for long. Calibrate targets against the measured crossing rate of the default orbit (headless run over minutes), not against the heat scale alone.
- A simulation that draws random numbers for gameplay (drifting targets, placement) must use a seeded generator. Otherwise two runs that should be bit-identical (the skip-forecast equivalence test) diverge.
